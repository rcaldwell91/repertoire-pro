# Repertoire — Train review plan (4 Nov)

This is a plan only: nothing in the app has changed. It sets your approved review, "Repertoire — Train exercise review (4 Nov)", against Train as it is on main now. Main's latest change is commit e0294ca, which only added a report. The app itself is exactly the 3 Nov build, commit b694551.

Your rules for Train apply throughout: only what a singer can learn by reading plus the app's tools, plain words, and the app never says something happened unless it checked. Where the review says "keep today's text", the app's current words stay exactly. The decisions that are yours to make are at the end, and there are five.

## 1. What changes across Train

- **The words.** Every exercise gets the review's steps, "doing it right", "watch out" and "why", in the layout built on 3 Nov. Today's text stays wherever the review says so. The wording checks carry on as they are: no jargon in the plain line, You'll need or the steps; the other parts are checked for banned words only. I read every new step and plain line against the jargon list and found nothing on it. The build runs the check again, and anything it finds comes back to you rather than being reworded by me.
- **"Stop when it stops feeling easy; you don't have to finish the climb."** This goes as the last step of every exercise that climbs, except where its steps already say the same thing (the Octave-and-a-half arpeggio, Octave repeat and Three-note steps already do).
- **Rounds you didn't sing don't count.** A round counts if your voice was heard on any of its notes. Inside a counted round, a silent note is still a miss. See section 5, point 2, on why rounds rather than single notes.
- **Stopping early gives you a result.** Today, Close in the middle of an exercise throws everything away, so "you don't have to finish the climb" would cost the singer their score. Proposal: once at least one round has been sung, Close shows the result for the rounds sung, as the tension check already does. The result says how many rounds it counted.
- **Levels.** Lip trills, the five runs, Nine-tone scale, the four tongue twisters and Match the note get levels. They use the level row in the gear's sheet built on 3 Nov, remembered for each exercise. The list row shows the span ("Beginner to Advanced").
- **Numbers to watch grow:** your best Steady hiss, your last range next to the new one, a goal time for Vocal Function Exercises, and how close your first try lands on Match the note.
- **Notes and the microphone** for Tongue-out with a crescendo and Blah. **Your line on screen** for Hold the note, which today shows no line at all (see Pitch below).
- **Three new exercises:** Neck and shoulder release, Three-note steps and Match the tone. That brings Train to 58.

## 3. How I'd build each new behaviour

1. **Speed levels** (runs, Nine-tone scale, twisters). Each level is the same pattern at its own speed, in the level row built on 3 Nov. Nothing else about how they run changes.
2. **Match the note's three levels.**
   - **Beginner:** 10 notes in order, a half step apart, from one note above the bottom of your range.
     - Each note plays (1.4 s, as today), then the app listens with no time limit.
     - Once you've held it within a half step for 1.2 seconds in all, the bubble fills, "Held" shows, and the next note plays a second later. That's today's rule and the old app's.
     - You can tap Next at any time. A note skipped without singing doesn't count. A note you sang but didn't land is a miss.
     - Pause and Resume play the note again.
   - **Intermediate:** today's random notes, with 5 seconds to land each instead of 2.4. The rule for a hit stays exactly as today: 1.2 seconds within a half step. On today's 2.4-second bubble that was "half of it"; on the 5-second one the fill shows progress toward the 1.2 seconds. A turn takes about 7.4 s, a round of ten about 75 s.
   - **Advanced:** the note plays; you sing for 3 seconds with your line hidden (the note's bubble still shows where it is); then the note plays again and your line from that attempt appears over its bubble. It's scored on the hidden attempt only. A turn is about 7.5 s.
   - The level is picked in the gear's sheet and remembered. The Daily warm-up uses Beginner.
3. **Lip trills levels:** as above, a pattern per level, all at 100.
4. **A level per routine step:** each step in a routine names its level. The table in Routines lists every step whose notes change.
5. **The Cool-down stepping down:** inside the Cool-down, the ladder starts at the middle of your range and moves down a half step a round to one above the bottom. At the default range: Straw 10 rounds, Humming 9, Descending five-tone 9.
6. **Not counting what wasn't sung:** a round with no voice on any of its notes is left out of the score, and the result says how many rounds it counted. For Match the note, Sing the interval, Hold the note and Match the tone, each turn or note counts the same way. The saved result holds only what was counted.
7. **The plain tone for Match the tone.**
   - The phone makes the tone itself each time, from a pure wave with a soft start and end so it doesn't click. It's handed to the player the same way a piano note is, so no sound file goes in the repo and the player itself doesn't change. Its loudness is matched to the piano.
   - 10 notes: five in order, a whole step apart from the lower middle of your range, then five drawn at random. Each plays for 10 seconds: 2 seconds to find it, then 8 seconds held with the tone still playing. It's scored like Hold the note (70% of the hold within 35 hundredths of a half step), and each waits for Next.
   - **Line shown, not scored, until your headphone test,** as the review says.
   - **A guard against a false line:** without headphones the microphone hears the tone, and "your line" would really be the tone. A pure tone is perfectly steady and a voice never is. So if the line sits dead on the tone with no movement at all for over a second, the screen says "I can hear the tone. Put your headphones in." That's tested on your phone with and without headphones before it ships.
   - Proposal: Hear yourself is on by default for this one, because the wobble is easiest to hear when your voice and the tone are both in your ears.
8. **Farinelli counts growing:** in, pause and out each last 4 seconds in round 1, 5 in round 2, and so on up to 8, then stay at 8 until Done. The screen shows the round's count.
9. **Best time on Steady hiss:** your best comes from your own saved results, on the phone and on your account. The database already lets a singer read their own results; no change there.
   - The result shows "Your best: 24 seconds".
   - "A new best." shows only when there was an earlier time and this one beat it.
   - The first time, it says it's your first.
10. **The old range on Range check:** before a new range is saved, the one it replaces is kept. The result then shows "Was A2 to E4." under the new range. This shows only when the old one was found by singing, not when it was the "Not measured yet" default.
11. **The Vocal Function Exercises goal:** your best Steady hiss, shown during the "ee" and "ol" holds ("Goal: 24 s") and in the result.
    - If you haven't done Steady hiss yet: "Do Steady hiss once to set your goal."
    - **Progress:** under the result, your longest "ee" on each day you did it, from your saved results (the last 10 such days).
12. **Landing measures on Match the note,** worked out for each note from your line:
    - **First try:** where your voice sits in its first 0.3 seconds.
    - **Time to settle:** from your first sound until you stay within 35 hundredths of a half step for 0.6 seconds.
    - **How close it settles:** your middle reading once settled.

    Shown in plain words: "First try on the note: 4 of 10 (last time 2)", "Took 1.8 seconds to settle, on average (last time 2.5)", and how close: "very close", "close" or "a bit off". "On the note" means within half a half step, the same rule as Vocal Function Exercises' "On the note". It's shown against your last three sessions.

    Saved with the result: the first-try average goes in the results table's existing "cents" column (the old app keeps its ear results the same way). The time to settle goes in "seconds", and notes landed in the score. "How close it settles" has no column. Question 4.
13. **Tongue-out with a crescendo and Blah moving onto notes:**
    - **Tongue-out with a crescendo** becomes five held notes, 8 seconds each on "ah", scored like Sustained note. It no longer uses a two-minute clock.
    - **Blah** becomes a five-note-scale ladder at 110 on "blah", the line shown but not scored. It no longer uses a one-minute clock.
14. **Three-note steps:** Pitch, Beginner, Quiet, 1-2-3-2-1 on "ah" at 110, climbing a half step every round, scored.
15. **Neck and shoulder release:** Warm-up, Beginner, Quiet, steps and a one-minute clock (see question 1).
16. **Song cues on Sing the interval:** one line under the jump, for the first three times you meet each shape (counted on the phone), then gone. Question 3 has the list.

Then, as every round: the old app read first where it had the same thing (rule 6), the checklist, tests for each new behaviour, broken builds to prove the tests can fail, all the suites, merge, and checking the live app.

## 4. The two scoring checks with a real voice

**The real voice.** In the lab, it's the singer from a real song recording. I use the separated voice that Train's checks already use, kept outside the repo. Real sung sounds are cut from it, each with its own natural start and end, and moved onto each note's pitch. It is never a pure tone. Then it's your voice on your phone (question 5).

**A. Very short notes: Staccato and Consonant pops**

- **Why it's a question.** A note counts when half its length is sung on pitch:
  - Staccato at 120 a minute has half-second and one-second notes. They need 0.25 s and 0.5 s on pitch.
  - Consonant pops are a third of a second each and need 0.15 s.
  - So a correct staccato note sung short on purpose, say 0.15 s, can never count, however true it is. That's arithmetic, not a guess.
- **Lab check:** every note sung right, at lengths of 0.1, 0.15, 0.2 and 0.3 seconds. Then the same sung three half steps off.
- **The fix, if (as I expect) the short right ones miss:** for these two exercises, a note counts when at least 0.1 seconds of it is heard and most of what's heard is within a half step. Nothing heard means not sung.
- **Pass:** the right voice lands 9 notes in 10 or better at every length from 0.1 s, and the off voice 1 in 10 or fewer. The consonants (p, b, t, d) must not throw the reading.

**B. Runs at speed**

- At 160 a minute each note lasts 0.375 seconds and needs 0.19 seconds on pitch.
- **Lab check:** all five runs and the Nine-tone scale at every level (100, 130, 160), sung right and sung off.
- **The lab voice's limit:** it moves from note to note at once, but a real singer slides between notes. So I also run a version where every note begins with a real short slide cut from the same recording.
- **Pass:** the right voice lands 85% or better and the off voice 15% or less, at every speed.
- **What a lab pass can't prove:** that real singers' runs score fairly. That needs your phone check.

**If a check fails and no fix passes,** that exercise or level ships with the line shown but not scored, and I'll say so.

**Your phone, after the build:** sing Staccato, Consonant pops and the Five-note run at 160, and tap Save take on each. I read your line from those takes and compare it with what you tell me you sang. Scores the lab passed stay on unless your takes show they're wrong.

## 5. Things in the review I think are wrong for a singer

1. **The hand on the head (Neck and shoulder release).** Even "let its weight draw" invites pulling, and the old app's research-based neck stretch refused hands for that reason. Suggested step 3: "Tip your head toward the opposite armpit and let it hang there for three slow breaths. Then the other side." Question 1.
2. **Notes versus rounds.** The decided call is headed "Rounds you didn't sing don't count", but the sentence under it says notes. If single silent notes didn't count, a singer could skip the top note of every round and score 100%. The app would then say they sang it all on pitch, which it didn't check. I'll build rounds: an unsung round is left out, and a silent note inside a sung round is a miss. If you meant notes, tell me.
3. **"Until the piano stops" (Sustained note).** The piano fades out after about 3 seconds of an 8-second hold. Suggested: "until its bubble ends".
4. **"Repeat it four times" (Octave repeat)** reads as five top notes. Suggested wording is in Range above.
5. **The 100 levels on Chromatic run and Three-note turns.** Slow is a good way to learn them, but a beginner landing half steps is hard at any speed. Question 2.
6. **Getting louder on high notes with the tongue out (Tongue-out with a crescendo).** Today's held notes reach three notes below your top, and a crescendo there, with the tongue out, is where people push. Suggested: for this exercise only, keep the five notes at least five half steps below your top.

Nothing in the review needs a demonstration the app can't give, in my reading. The closest is Match the tone's "wobble", and the steps describe it well enough to listen for.
